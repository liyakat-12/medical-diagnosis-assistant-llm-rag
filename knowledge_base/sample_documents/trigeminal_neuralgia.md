# Trigeminal Neuralgia

Trigeminal neuralgia causes sudden electric-shock-like stabs of pain in one side of the face, lasting seconds, triggered by chewing, brushing teeth, talking, or a light breeze.

Ordinary painkillers rarely work. A clinician prescribes nerve-pain medicine such as carbamazepine, and surgery is an option if tablets fail.

See a doctor for repeated shocks of facial pain. Seek urgent care if there is also facial numbness, weakness, or double vision.
