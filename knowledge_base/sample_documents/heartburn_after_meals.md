# Heartburn After Meals

A burning rise behind the breastbone after a large, spicy, or late meal is typical heartburn from acid reflux. People may also taste sour fluid. It is common and not automatically a heart attack, but chest pain still needs care if it is new or severe.

Eat smaller meals, avoid lying down for three hours after food, raise the head of the bed, and limit coffee, alcohol, and very fatty food. Pharmacy antacids may help if they are suitable for you.

See a doctor if heartburn happens most weeks or you have trouble swallowing. Seek emergency care for crushing chest pain, pain into the arm or jaw, or breathlessness.
